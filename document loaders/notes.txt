STATISTICS: CORRELATION AND REGRESSION
======================================

1. CORRELATION
--------------
Definition:
Correlation measures the degree and direction of linear relationship between two variables.

Types:
- Positive correlation: Both variables increase together.
- Negative correlation: One variable increases while the other decreases.
- Zero correlation: No linear relationship.

Methods to Measure:
- Pearson’s correlation coefficient (r):
  Formula: r = Σ[(xi - x̄)(yi - ȳ)] / √[Σ(xi - x̄)² * Σ(yi - ȳ)²]
  Range: -1 ≤ r ≤ +1
- Spearman’s rank correlation:
  Formula: r_s = 1 - (6 Σd²) / [n(n² - 1)]
  Used for ranked data.

Interpretation:
- r = +1 : Perfect positive linear relationship
- r = -1 : Perfect negative linear relationship
- r ≈ 0 : No linear relationship

Limitations:
- Only measures linear relationships.
- Sensitive to outliers.

2. REGRESSION
-------------
Definition:
Regression estimates the relationship between a dependent variable (Y) and one or more independent variables (X).

Types:
- Simple linear regression: One independent variable.
- Multiple regression: Two or more independent variables.

Simple Linear Regression Equation:
Y = a + bX
Where:
- a = intercept
- b = slope (rate of change in Y per unit change in X)

Calculation:
b = Σ[(xi - x̄)(yi - ȳ)] / Σ(xi - x̄)²
a = ȳ - b * x̄

Interpretation:
- Slope (b): Direction and magnitude of relationship.
- Intercept (a): Value of Y when X = 0.

Coefficient of Determination (R²):
- Measures proportion of variance in Y explained by X.
- Range: 0 to 1.

Assumptions:
- Linearity
- Independence of errors
- Homoscedasticity (constant variance)
- Normality of residuals

Differences between Correlation and Regression:
- Correlation: Strength & direction of relationship; no causation implied.
- Regression: Predicts Y from X; implies directional relationship.

Applications:
- Forecasting
- Quality control
- Economics
- Social sciences
